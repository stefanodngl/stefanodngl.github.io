Methodology

The following page is dedicated to explaining the characteristics of the simulator related to its construction, the data gathered and the calculation to produce the results.
Bank list
The list of all banks operating in Italy has been retrieved from the lists of banks of the Bank of Italy and the Bank of Spain. For each bank listed in the dataset, the research extracted data from the information sheet (in Italian, “Foglio informativo”) of the corresponding product. Banks operating in Italy are subject to the publication of clear and transparent information related to each product offered following the Consolidated Law on Banking (in Italian, “Testo Unico Bancario” – Legislative Decree 385/1993 and following).
Products and time periods
The product included in the datasheet refers to deposit account only (in Italian, “Conto deposito” or “Deposito bancario vincolato”). The product included in the datasheet refers to deposits having fixed interest rates with 3-, 6-, 9-, 12-, 18-, 30-, 36-, 48-, and 60-month investment periods, being the most commonly offered time period for deposit accounts.
Terms update
When gathering data related to interest rates, the datasheet reports the most recent data available on the product terms.
Multiple deposit products
When a bank offers similar deposit account products, the datasheet reports data on the product with least expensive conditions (opening, closing, commissions) or with the highest interest rate offered. To make a fair comparison between standard deposit products, the datasheet does not include temporal offers (e.g. higher interest rates for limited time periods) or products with limiting conditions (e.g. products available to associates only, in case of cooperative banks).
Chart display
The graph displays the results related to the following investment periods: 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 4 years, and 5 years.
Calculation
To calculate the net interest of the deposit account the simulator uses the following equation:
\((C*i) * ({l \over 12})\)
\(- [(C*i) * ({l \over 12}) * 0.26] \)
\(- (C*s)* ({l \over 12}) \)
where \(C\) is the amount invested, \(i\) is the gross annual interest rate, \(l\) is the deposit period in months, and \(s\) is the stamp tax which is equal to 0,2% when paid by the client, or 0 when paid by the bank.
Taxes
The Italian taxation system imposes two different taxes on deposit accounts. The first one is the capital gains tax, which amounts to 26% of the gross profit from the investment. This tax is deducted and collected directly by the bank when paying interests to the investor. The second tax is the stamp tax (in Italian, “imposta di bollo”), which amounts to 0,20% of the total amount invested per year. The tax is commonly paid directly to the bank at the beginning or at the end of the investment period, unless stated otherwise on the information sheet of the product. To date, only 5 banks offer to pay the stamp tax on behalf of the investor.
Additional costs
The majority of the deposit account offered by banks operating in Italy does not entail any cost connected to the deposit account. However, some banks charge additional costs for deposit accounts, mainly related to the opening, management and closing of the deposit account. The opening cost (in Italian, “spese di apertura/accensione vincolo”) refers to a fixed amount charged at the moment of opening the deposit account. The management cost (in Italian, “spese di tenuta conto/conto”) refers to a fixed amount charged yearly for the management of the deposit account. Lastly, the closing cost (in Italian, “spese di chiusura/estinzione vincolo”) refers to a fixed amount charged at the moment of closing the deposit account. In addition, no bank currently charges any commission (in Italian, “commissioni”) on the deposit account. Since the vast majority of banks do not apply any additional costs related to deposit account and in order to avoid too complex calculations given by the different nature of additional costs (e.g. costs of bank statement, additional charges), the net interest calculated by the simulator does not include additional costs in the computation.
